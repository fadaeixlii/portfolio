## The Problem

Real estate markets are notoriously opaque. Investors, analysts, and even homeowners have strong opinions about where property values are heading, but there's no mechanism to put conviction behind those opinions or aggregate collective intelligence into actionable signals. Traditional forecasting relies on historical data and expert models — it doesn't capture the distributed knowledge of thousands of market participants who each see a piece of the picture.

Prediction markets solve this for politics and sports. The question was: could the same mechanism work for real estate, where outcomes are slower, more nuanced, and harder to settle?

## The Approach

I built Roofcast as a Web3 DApp on Polygon using Gnosis Conditional Tokens — a battle-tested framework for creating and settling prediction markets on-chain. The choice of Polygon over Ethereum mainnet was driven by gas costs: prediction markets involve many small transactions (placing bets, claiming winnings), and mainnet fees would have made micro-positions uneconomical.

Gnosis Conditional Tokens provided the core market mechanics: creating conditions (e.g., "Will property prices in Amsterdam-Zuid increase by >5% in 2025?"), minting outcome tokens, and settling markets in USDC based on oracle-provided results. Positions are threshold-based — users stake on whether a property value crosses a defined level — and winnings settle on-chain in USDC. I built the smart contracts, the settlement logic, and the frontend that made this accessible to users who might never have interacted with a DApp before.

The backend runs NestJS with PostgreSQL, handling user profiles, market metadata, historical data, and the off-chain components that support the on-chain logic — like price feed aggregation and notification services.

## The Solution

The **market creation flow** lets authenticated users propose new prediction markets. Each market defines a question, a set of outcomes, a resolution date, and a resolution source (which oracle or data feed will determine the outcome). Markets go through a review process before going live to prevent spam and ensure resolvability.

The **trading interface** shows live markets with current odds, volume, and time remaining. Users connect their wallet via WalletConnect, approve token spending, and place positions by purchasing outcome tokens. The UI shows real-time price movements as other participants trade — driven by WebSocket connections to the NestJS backend that monitors on-chain events.

The **Gnosis Conditional Token integration** was the most complex piece. Each market creates a condition on-chain. When a user takes a position, the contract mints conditional tokens representing their bet. If the condition resolves in their favor, they can redeem tokens for the underlying collateral. The settlement process uses a combination of on-chain oracles and a manual resolution mechanism for markets where automated data isn't available.

**Wallet interaction** was designed to minimize Web3 friction. Users connect once via WalletConnect, and subsequent transactions use a session-based approval flow. The UI clearly shows gas estimates, transaction status, and provides plain-language explanations of what each on-chain action does — critical for users who understand real estate but not blockchain.

The **data layer** combines on-chain state (market positions, settlements) with off-chain data (market descriptions, user profiles, historical property data). PostgreSQL stores the off-chain components, while the frontend reads on-chain state directly from Polygon via Ethers.js. A background indexer keeps the off-chain database in sync with on-chain events for fast querying.

## Impact

The platform demonstrated that prediction market mechanics work for real estate — a domain with longer time horizons and more complex outcomes than typical prediction markets. The Polygon deployment kept transaction costs low enough for meaningful micro-positions. The WalletConnect integration and plain-language UI reduced the Web3 barrier for real estate professionals who were new to DApps.

## Reflections

The biggest challenge was market design, not code. Defining questions that are specific enough to be resolvable but broad enough to attract liquidity is an art. "Will Amsterdam prices go up?" is too vague. "Will the Kadaster index for Amsterdam-Zuid postal codes 1071-1079 increase by >5% YoY in Q4 2025?" is specific but might not attract enough participants. Finding the right granularity required iteration and user feedback. The technical stack — Polygon, Gnosis CT, NestJS — was solid and well-documented. The hard part was the product, not the engineering.
